# Facilitator script

Read only the participant-facing passages aloud. Keep the key and expected outcomes out of the participant view. Do not send messages to recruits or collect material automatically.

## Opening and consent

> We are evaluating this pre-release tool, not you. Think aloud if comfortable. You may skip any task, withhold content, or stop at any time. We will take coded task notes, not collect your page, source, URLs, Locators, reports, screenshots, or recordings. Is observing your interaction with these synthetic interfaces acceptable?

Record participation consent separately according to the privacy protocol. If declined, stop. Explain only study logistics and the ability to stop; do not define Scenario, Finding, Evidence, subject, coverage, or Restore in advance. Keep a timer ready, not an automated page tracker.

## Controlled phase

Open the assigned neutral fixture in the predetermined order. Give this one general goal for each page:

> Explore how this interface handles more demanding text using UI Torture Lab. Use the result to decide what you would investigate for a code review, prepare something useful to share if appropriate, and leave the page in a state you are comfortable with. Tell me what you are thinking as you go.

Start the timer when giving the goal. Do not point to the toolbar, select a Scenario, choose an element, explain a Finding, identify Evidence, suggest Restore timing, or tell the participant which outcome to expect. Observe the first self-directed attempt. If they ask for help, say:

> What would you try based on what you see?

If they remain blocked, record the blockage and any requested help. Offer to skip or continue trying. Any substantive rescue coaching marks that task assisted; it cannot count as independent success. Stop the timed task before later explanation.

After their attempt, use neutral probes:

- What does this result tell you, and what does it leave uncertain?
- What changed, and what are you comparing it with?
- Where would you start investigating in your own code, and why?
- What do you think the highlighted thing represents?
- What state is the page in now? How certain are you about that?
- Would the text prepared for sharing help a review? What would you edit before sharing?

Do not correct answers until the primary observation is recorded. Record whether the participant notices a coverage limitation without being told. Never infer success merely because a button was clicked or a Finding existed.

## Participant-owned phase

> If you have an interface you own, can modify, and are authorized to inspect, you may try the same goal there. It can stay entirely on your computer. Do not give us access, a URL, code, screenshots, or the report. You can describe your experience without showing sensitive content, or skip this phase.

The project receives no page access. No remote control, application deployment, data submission, or credential entry is part of the task. Observe only with voluntary permission; stop or switch to verbal reflection if confidential material appears. Do not suggest a Scenario, explain the result, or ask for a pasted report. Record the interface only as a coarse class (for example, local prototype or authorized staging).

## Debrief

- What would you do next after this result?
- Which words or states were unclear?
- What made the result trustworthy or difficult to trust?
- Did Restore behave as you expected? What would reload-required mean to you?
- Would you use the Markdown in a review? What information was missing or unnecessary?
- Did the panel or highlights interfere with the interface or your investigation?

Only after recording interpretation, clarify that Possible Cause is a hypothesis, Findings have no severity, and no Findings is not proof of correctness. This explanation is debriefing, never evidence of unassisted comprehension. Confirm the participant leaves pages safely restored or explicitly reloads where required. Thank them, reiterate withdrawal/deletion arrangements, and do not ask for raw artifacts.
